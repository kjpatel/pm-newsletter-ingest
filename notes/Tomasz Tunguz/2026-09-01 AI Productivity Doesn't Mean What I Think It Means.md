# AI Productivity Doesn't Mean What I Think It Means

**Source**: [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/ai-productivity-doesnt-mean-what-i-think/)
**Author**: Tomasz Tunguz | **Date**: Sep 01, 2026

---

## Summary

AI productivity doesn't mean doing less work—it means raising the ceiling of output quality by automating mechanical tasks and freeing human effort to focus on higher-value work. Using a closed-loop taste flywheel for writing, the author found that while structural edits collapsed from 47 to 3 drafts, line-level edits remained consistent at ~130 per post, redirecting attention upward to rhetorical precision rather than eliminating effort entirely.

## Key Takeaways

- **Shift your mental model**: Stop expecting AI to reduce human effort to zero. Instead, measure productivity by how much better the output becomes with the same or similar time investment.
- **Build closed-loop systems with memory**: One-shot prompts fail. Create AI agents that ingest background research, learn from human feedback, and log lessons into persistent files so each iteration improves based on accumulated taste rules.
- **Move up the value chain**: Once AI handles structural scaffolding, redirect your effort from fixing broken arguments to line-level craft—sentence rhythm, antitheses, and rhetorical precision matter more than ever.
- **Accept the higher bar or stagnate**: Like chess engines that raised the ceiling for human players without requiring more training hours, AI makes higher-quality work possible. The choice is whether you accept pre-AI baseline quality or push for the new ceiling.
